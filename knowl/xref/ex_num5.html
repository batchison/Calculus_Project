Example 1.5.18. Using Simpson’s Rule.

Approximate \(\ds\int_0^1 e^{-x^2}\, dx\) using Simpson’s Rule and 4 equally spaced subintervals.
🔗
Solution.
We begin by making a table of values as we have in the past, as shown in Figure 1.5.19(a).
🔗
Simpson’s Rule states that
\begin{equation*} \int_0^1e^{-x^2}\, dx \approx \frac{0.25}{3}\Big[1+4(0.939)+2(0.779)+4(0.570) + 0.368\Big] = 0.7468\overline{3}\text{.} \end{equation*}
🔗
Recall in Example 1.5.2 we stated that the correct answer, accurate to 4 places after the decimal, was 0.7468. Our approximation with Simpson’s Rule, with 4 subintervals, is better than our approximation with the Trapezoidal Rule using 5!
🔗
Figure 1.5.19(b) shows \(f(x) = e^{-x^2}\) along with its approximating parabolas, demonstrating how good our approximation is. The approximating curves are nearly indistinguishable from the actual function.
