    Chapter 2 Derivatives

    Chapter 1 introduced the most fundamental of calculus topics: the limit. This chapter introduces the second most fundamental of calculus topics: the derivative. Limits describe where a function is going; derivatives describe how fast the function is going.
    🔗

    The graph of a piecewise-linear function. For \(x\lt -1\) it has slope \(3\text{.}\) It meets the \(x\) axis at \((-2,0)\) and continues to \((-1,3)\text{.}\) At \(x=-1\) the graph bends, and it has slope \(-1\) for \(x\gt -1\text{.}\) From \((-1,3)\) the line passes through \((0,2)\) and then \((2,0)\text{.}\)
    🔗

    9.

    
    The graph appears to be a cubic function. From left to right, it begins below the \(y\) axis and travels upward, crossing the \(x\) axis to the left of \((-1,0)\text{.}\) The graph meets the \(y\) axis at \((0,5)\text{,}\) and reaches a peak at point where \(x\) is somewhere between \(0\) and \(1\text{.}\) The graph then descends, crossing the \(x\) axis again around \(2.5\text{,}\) until reaching a valley when \(x\) is a little less than \(4\text{.}\) From there the graph travels upward again for the remainder of the image.
    🔗
    🔗

    10.

    
    The graph of what looks like a rational function is plotted relative to \(x\) and \(y\) coordinate axes. However, it cannot be a rational function, as it has two horizontal asymptotes. As \(x\to \infty^-\text{,}\) the graph approaches \(y=-3\) from above. Moving left to right, the graph increases steadily and approaches \(\infty\) along a vertical asymptote at \(x=-1\text{.}\)
    🔗
    On the right side of the vertical asymptote, the graph approaches \(-\infty\text{.}\) It then climbs steeply toward a \(y\) intercept at about \(y=2\text{.}\) After that, the graph levels off and approaches the horizontal asymptote \(y=3\) from below.
